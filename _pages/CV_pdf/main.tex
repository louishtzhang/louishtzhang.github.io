\section{Working Papers}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\href{https://www.researchgate.net/publication/417864546_Estimation_and_Inference_for_Spot_Variance_of_Microstructure_Noise?_tp=eyJjb250ZXh0Ijp7InBhZ2UiOiJwcm9maWxlIiwicHJldmlvdXNQYWdlIjoicHVibGljYXRpb24iLCJwb3NpdGlvbiI6InBhZ2VDb250ZW50Iiwic3ViUGFnZSI6Im92ZXJ2aWV3In19}
    {Estimation and Inference for Spot Variance of Microstructure Noise}} (\textit{Under Review at \textit{Journal of American Statistical Association}})\\
    with Z. Merrick Li.
    \begin{itemize}[label=$\circ$, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=0.6em, topsep=0.3em]
        \item  Presented at (\textbf{Bold text} = presented by me): 
        First Macau International Conference on Business Intelligence and Analytics, 
        University of Macau, Macau (Dec 2024); 
        The 19th International Symposium on Econometric Theory and Applications (SETA), 
        University of Macau, Macau (June 2025); 
        \textbf{17th SoFiE Conference, ESSEC, 
        France (July 2025, awarded with PhD/Postdoc travel funding); 
        2025 Econometric Society World Congress, Seoul, South Korea (August 2025)}; 
        Workshop on Financial Econometric, University of Macau, Macau (Jan 2026).
        \item \textbf{Abstract:} We develop estimation and inference for the spot variance of market microstructure
        noise, the discrepancy between observed transaction prices and underlying efficient
        prices arising from trading frictions. The noise is a stationary, serially dependent
        sequence scaled by the square root of a positive semimartingale that may jump at
        prescheduled times. Using transaction prices alone, we construct one-sided estimators of
        the left and right limits of the noise variance from a multi-horizon differencing
        scheme; averaging over a growing set of horizons renders them asymptotically equivalent
        to the oracle that observes the noise. We establish a joint stable central limit theorem
        and provide consistent asymptotic-variance estimators and a test for scheduled jumps. In
        SPY transactions over 2016--2025, the noise variance jumps at macroeconomic releases and
        closing-auction deadlines with event-specific recovery dynamics.\\
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Uniform Inference for Intraday Market Frictions} (Job Market Paper)
    \begin{itemize}[label=$\circ$, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=0.6em, topsep=0.3em]
        \item  Presented at: 
        First Research Colloquium in Financial Econometrics, 
        ESSEC Business School, France (May 2026). 
        \item \textbf{Abstract:} We develop spot and uniform tests for market microstructure noise that remain valid
        when the efficient price exhibits drift bursts. 
        Noise and drift bursts move the 
        autocovariance of high-frequency returns in opposite directions, 
        so each can offset the other. 
        We show that a multi-horizon autocovariance test loses size and power once
        the drift is strong enough. 
        We propose a test based on local demeaning of the returns which 
        restores the stable central limit theorem over a wide range of drift intensities 
        and preserves power against the existence of the spot noise,
        even when the noise is locally stationary, 
        serially dependent, and with shrinking magnitude. 
        Moreover, we establish a strong Gaussian approximation 
        for the minimum of the spot statistics over a growing number of disjoint windows 
        to derive critical values for uniform inference over the trading day.
        Simulations support the theory, and we illustrate the tests with transaction prices of
        four exchange-traded funds, including SPY on the day of the 2010 Flash Crash.\\
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{A ReMeDI for Stale Prices}\\[0.3em]
    with Federico M. Bandi, Z. Merrick Li, and Roberto Ren\`o.
    \begin{itemize}[label=$\circ$, leftmargin=2.2em, itemsep=0.6em, topsep=0.3em]
        \item \textbf{Abstract:} Stale prices---trades that repeat the last recorded price while quotes
        move---distort the measurement of how long transitory pricing errors
        persist, a quantity central to liquidity measurement, noise-robust
        volatility estimation, and the speed of price discovery. 
        We show that there exists a carry-forward effect which can generate a 
        supurious autocorrelation of the pricing errors even when the pricing errors 
        are actually serially independent. 
        To solve this problem, we propose a new estimator of 
        the persistence of the pricing errors when the observed prices 
        are stale. We establish a stable central limit theorem for the new estimator, 
        and provide a feasible estimator of the asymptotic variance in purpose of inference. 
        We compare the new estimator with the existing estimators in the literature using 
        the transaction prices of components of the Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) 
        index in 2017, and find that the pricing error is far less persistent 
        than previously thought if we correctly account for the staleness. 
    \end{itemize}
    
\end{itemize}
